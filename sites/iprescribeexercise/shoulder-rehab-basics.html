Shoulder Rehab Basics

POSTED AUGUST 3, 2026 BY I Prescribe Exercise

Have you ever pressed through a "twinge" in your shoulder because the set felt too good to rack?

That twinge is exactly how mine started. It took eight months of overhead pressing through warning signs before my shoulder finally forced the issue, and another year of rehab to trust it under a bar again. Here's the habit that wrecked it, and the three checks I now run before every pressing session so it doesn't happen twice.

What Actually Wrecked My Shoulder

It wasn't one rep. It was months of pressing heavier than my shoulder's mobility could support, then bracing through the pinch at the top instead of backing off. By the time it gave out, the damage wasn't a single tear I could point to. It was a joint that had been compensating for so long it forgot what a clean overhead path felt like.

Skipping these things made it worse, not better:

  • Pressing heavy before warming up rotator cuff and scapular control
  • Chasing a locked-out top position my shoulder mobility didn't actually have
  • Treating a pinch as "just tightness" instead of a setup problem
  • Never deloading, even after weeks of the same twinge showing up

The Three Checks I Run Before Every Pressing Session

1. Can I Reach an Honest Overhead Position?

Before any weight goes overhead, I check whether my arm can actually get there without my lower back arching to fake the range. If it can't, the session becomes a mobility session, not a pressing session. No exceptions, no matter how the program looks on paper.

2. Is the Pinch Gone, or Just Ignored?

A few band pull-aparts and prone Y-raises tell me the difference fast. If the pinch is still there after warming up the cuff and upper back, that's the shoulder telling me today isn't a heavy day, and I've stopped arguing with it.

3. Am I Programming a Deload, or Waiting for an Injury to Force One?

Every fourth week is lighter, on purpose, whether or not anything hurts yet. That single change has done more for my shoulder's longevity than any single exercise I've added.

Where This Leaves You

None of this is exotic. It's the boring stuff I skipped because it slowed my training down. Run the three checks before your next pressing session, and if the honest answer is no on any of them, that's your program for the day.

And if your shoulder's already past "twinge" and into something that won't settle, that's a physical therapist's job, not a blog post's. Check out the rest of the injury guides for what to do while you wait for that appointment.
