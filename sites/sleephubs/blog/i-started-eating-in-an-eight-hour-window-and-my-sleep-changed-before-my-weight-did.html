I Started Eating In An Eight Hour Window, And My Sleep Changed Before My Weight Did

I Started Eating In An Eight Hour Window, And My Sleep Changed Before My Weight Did

I've tried cutting out almost everything at some point, caffeine, alcohol, sugar, my phone before bed. Somewhere in the middle of all that self experimenting I kept running into people talking about intermittent fasting, not for the weight loss angle everyone else was chasing, but for what it did to their sleep. That caught my attention in a way the diet claims never did, because sleep was the one thing none of my other experiments had fully cracked.

So I picked the version most people start with, the 16:8 method, eating everything within an eight hour window and fasting for the other sixteen. I settled on eating between 9 in the morning and 5 in the afternoon, which meant no dinner in the usual sense and definitely no evening snacking. My plan was simple. Track what actually happened to my sleep, not just my weight, and see if changing when I ate mattered more than what I ate.

I didn't expect the first stretch to go the way it did.

What Changed Once I Started Eating On A Schedule

  1. Falling asleep stopped taking so long. Before this I was regularly lying there for 30 to 60 minutes most nights, running through the day in my head. Once I closed the kitchen by 5, that dropped to something closer to 10 or 15 minutes. I wasn't trying to fall asleep faster. It just started happening.
  2. Fewer trips to the bathroom overnight. I used to wake up somewhere between 2 and 5 times a night, half of it bathroom runs. That dropped to 1 or 2 wake ups, and most nights I wasn't getting up to use the bathroom at all.
  3. The stuffy, overfull feeling at bedtime disappeared. I hadn't noticed how uncomfortable I usually was in bed until it was gone. No more lying there feeling too warm and stuffed, and none of the rapid heartbeat or sweating I used to get some nights after a late dinner.
  4. My cravings didn't disappear, they just moved to the morning. The hunger I expected at night showed up instead right before my eating window opened. A cup of coffee first thing took the edge off enough that I could wait for real food.
  5. Hydration turned into its own project. I had to actually think about water instead of just reaching for it whenever. Water, plain tea, and black coffee got me through the fasting hours, and I made a point of cutting off fluids a few hours before bed so I wasn't trading fewer food related wake ups for more bathroom ones.
  6. The brain fog lifted, and not just at night. Within the first few days I noticed I was thinking more clearly during the day, not just sleeping better at night. Whatever was happening with my sleep seemed to be carrying over into how sharp I felt while I was awake.
  7. None of this showed up without a rough start first. The version of this that looks good on a list left out how badly the first several nights actually went.

How It Actually Went, Night By Night

The First Few Nights Were WORSE, Not Better

Nobody warns you that intermittent fasting can wreck your sleep before it fixes it. My first three nights were rougher than any caffeine withdrawal I've been through. I'd lie there wide awake even though I was tired enough to fall over, my heart rate a little higher than usual, my mind racing in a way that had nothing to do with anything specific I was thinking about. It turns out that's a hormonal thing. Changing when you eat pushes up certain stress hormones for a while, and until your body adjusts to the new schedule it can genuinely disrupt your sleep instead of helping it.

I almost quit on night two. I told myself if it wasn't better by the end of the week, I'd go back to eating whenever.

By The End Of Week One, The HUNGER Started Losing

The mornings were still hard. I'd wake up genuinely hungry before my window even opened, and that first cup of coffee became less of a preference and more of a survival tool. But the nights had already started to shift. I wasn't tossing and turning the way I had been. I was falling asleep faster than I expected to, even on the nights I went to bed still thinking about food.

I stopped forcing myself to bed at a set time and started actually waiting until I felt tired, which sounds obvious but wasn't something I'd been doing. Some nights that meant staying up a little later than usual. Most nights it meant I fell asleep faster than I had in months once I finally did lie down.

Somewhere In Week Two, I Started FALLING ASLEEP Before My Head Even Hit The Pillow

This was the part that actually surprised me. The 10 to 15 minute fall asleep time wasn't a single lucky night. It was becoming the normal one. I wasn't waking up sweaty or with my heart pounding anymore. The bathroom trips had thinned out to almost nothing. My husband noticed before I did, mostly because he stopped seeing me pass through the hallway at 2 in the morning.

I still don't fully understand every part of why it worked. Closing the kitchen hours before bed seems to give the body time to actually digest instead of splitting its attention between digesting and winding down for sleep, and keeping my blood sugar steadier through the evening meant I wasn't getting jolted awake by the kind of crash that used to hit me a few hours after a late dinner.

A Month In, I Wasn't Chasing Sleep Anymore

By the fourth week the hunger in the mornings had mostly settled down, and I wasn't thinking about food nearly as much during my fasting hours. I hadn't gotten sick once that whole stretch, which felt worth mentioning given how often I usually catch whatever's going around. Mostly though, I just stopped thinking about sleep as something I had to fight for every night. It had quietly become something that just happened once I lay down.

I'm still eating in that same 8 hour window months later, mostly because I never got a good enough reason to go back. If closing your kitchen a few hours earlier could change how fast you fall asleep, would you actually try it, or does the first rough week scare you off before you get there?

If you want to try this without diving straight into 16 hours, start smaller. A 12 hour or 14 hour fast is a gentler way to get your body used to the idea before you push the window down to 8. Keep drinking water and unsweetened tea through the fasting hours so you're not confusing thirst for hunger, and cut off fluids a few hours before bed so you're not just swapping one kind of wake up for another. And if you're managing a blood sugar condition, pregnant, or on medication that needs to be taken with food, this one is worth talking through with a doctor before you try it, not after.
