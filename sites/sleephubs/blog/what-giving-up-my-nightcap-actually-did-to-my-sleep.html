What Giving Up My Nightcap Actually Did To My Sleep

Sleep Hubs · 16 September 2026

What Giving Up My Nightcap Actually Did To My Sleep

For years my end of shift ritual was a glass of red once the kids were finally down, and a second most nights if I am honest. I called it my off switch. Chase a toddler all day, fight my body back onto a daytime schedule after years of rotating shifts, and by nine o'clock that glass felt earned. It also worked exactly the way it was supposed to. Within minutes of that last sip my eyes went heavy and I was gone.

What I never clocked for the longest time was what happened after that. I would wake up around three most mornings with my heart going too fast for no reason, lie there for what felt like an hour, then finally drop off again just in time for the alarm. My face looked puffy most mornings. Dry mouth, dark circles I could not blame on the toddler alone, and a headache that ibuprofen only half touched. I chalked all of it up to parenting a small kid and getting older. Turns out I was not even close.

I know what you are thinking, because I used to think it too. A drink helps you sleep, right? Turns out something like a third of adults reach for one specifically to fall asleep, and most of us are convinced it is actually working. I was one of them for years. So with a quiet couple of weeks lined up, I cut the nightcap completely and paid attention to what actually happened once it was gone, not what I assumed would happen. What I found did not match the story I had been telling myself at all.

What Changed Once The Nightcap Was Gone

  1. Falling asleep took longer, but staying asleep got easier. The wine used to knock me out within minutes. Without it, it took closer to twenty minutes most nights. But once I was under I mostly stayed under, instead of drifting in and out for the whole second half of the night the way I always had.
  2. The three in the morning wakeups just stopped. Alcohol wears off three to four hours after the last drink, and what follows is basically your body throwing stimulants at itself to counter the sedative. That is the racing heart and the wide awake feeling I used to blame on stress. Take the wine away and that whole cycle never starts.
  3. I stopped needing the bathroom in the middle of the night. Alcohol is a diuretic, so it was pulling fluid out of me all night and waking me to deal with it. Once I cut it, those trips mostly disappeared, and so did the groggy stumble back into bed after.
  4. My dreams came back, properly vivid ones. Alcohol blocks off a big chunk of the dreaming stage of sleep for roughly the first half of the night, so I had basically been skipping it for years without knowing. Within a couple of weeks I was waking up remembering full, strange, detailed dreams again, which I had honestly forgotten I used to have.
  5. The snoring stopped, according to my partner anyway. Alcohol relaxes the muscles in your throat and airway, which is why a couple of glasses always turned me into a snorer by the second half of the night. My partner noticed the quiet before I did.
  6. No more puffy morning face or dry mouth. The dark circles, the puffiness, the cotton mouth I had been blaming on my toddler and on getting older. Within about a week of stopping, most of that had cleared up on its own.
  7. It felt like addition again, not subtraction. When I quit caffeine later, it felt like losing a list of problems. This felt different. My skin got brighter, my energy came back in a way people at work actually commented on, and some of the weight I had been carrying came off without me trying. Cutting the nightcap did not feel like giving something up. It felt like getting something back.

How It Actually Went, Night By Night

The First Three Nights Were Just BRUTAL

I will not dress this part up. Some nights it took one to three hours to fall asleep, and one night I did not really fall asleep at all. My nervous system was on high alert the entire time, which made no sense to me because I was exhausted. Night sweats, restless legs that had me pacing the hallway at midnight, and a mind that would not stop racing over nothing in particular. Even the sleep I did manage came in small, broken pieces I could count.

What got me through was reminding myself this was my body rebounding from years of outsourcing the job to a glass of wine. It had gotten used to relying on alcohol's effects instead of producing its own calming chemicals in the evening, and now it had to remember how.

By Night Five The DREAMS Got Strange

Falling asleep eased up to somewhere between thirty and sixty minutes, which felt like a gift compared to the first three nights even though it was still nowhere near the ten minutes wine used to give me. The dreams got vivid, occasionally into nightmare territory, and my partner told me I had started talking in my sleep, which had never happened before. I was still waking up somewhere between three and five in the morning most nights and could not drop straight back off, and I was still dragging by early afternoon even though I was technically racking up more hours than before.

Week Two Was The TURNING POINT

This is where it actually started to click. Falling asleep dropped to fifteen or thirty minutes, and I was getting four to six hour stretches without waking up at all, which had not happened in longer than I could remember. The night sweats mostly stopped. The dreams were still vivid but had lost their edge, less nightmare and more just strange. I woke up less anxious and less foggy, most mornings.

By Week Four I Was Sleeping Like An Actual Adult Again

Deep sleep properly kicked back in. I was getting six to eight hours most nights instead of chasing it, my mood steadied out through the day, and my energy stopped crashing every afternoon. More mornings than not I was waking up naturally before the alarm even went off, which had not happened since before I had kids.

Would I go back to the nightly glass? Honestly, no. Not because I think a drink here and there is some terrible thing, but because I finally know what it was quietly costing me every single night for years while I told myself it was helping. If you have been assuming your own nightcap is doing you a favor, what is actually stopping you from testing that for two weeks and finding out for yourself?

If cutting it out completely feels like too much right now, you do not have to go cold turkey to see a difference. Stopping at least three hours before bed gives your body time to clear most of it before your head hits the pillow. Magnesium or a bit of L theanine about an hour before bed, or a cup of chamomile or valerian tea in place of the glass of wine, can cover a lot of the same relaxed feeling without asking your sleep to pay for it later.
